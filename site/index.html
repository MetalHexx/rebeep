A DOS TSR for PC-speaker games

Hear your beeper-only DOS games through a real sound card.

Plenty of 1980s DOS games can only beep: one square wave, through a tiny speaker inside the case. ReBeep catches those beeps and plays them on your Sound Blaster's FM chip instead.

01How it works

From one square wave to a whole FM voice

ReBeep sits between the game and the hardware. The game is never patched: it beeps as it always did, and the note comes out of the FM chip.

02See it

Meet ReBeep Studio

Shape your own FM voices in a full-screen DOS editor that comes with ReBeep. Play the keyboard while you edit, press F12 for a random sound or F11 to mutate it, then hear it in your games.

03Highlights

Your Sound Blaster, finally in the game

Load it before the game, and the beeps play on the card's FM chip instead of the tiny speaker inside the case.

Five voices, and your own

A faithful square wave close to the original, four FM patches, or one you shape in ReBeep Studio.

  • Square
  • Organ
  • Flute
  • Brass
  • Bell
  • Yours

Plays on the card you have

OPL2 or OPL3 FM on a Sound Blaster or most clones, on a real PC or in 86Box.

  • OPL2
  • OPL3
  • 388h

Change it while you play

Ctrl+Alt+B swaps the sound card and the speaker. Ctrl+Alt+N switches between the faithful voice and your patch.

Small and tidy

One .COM file that loads high and unloads cleanly. The game is never patched.

  • One file
  • Loads high
  • Unloads clean

04Get it

What you need, and three steps to install

What you need

CPU
A 386 or better
DOS
MS-DOS 5 or later, or FreeDOS
Memory
Jemm 5.87 or later, with its QPIEMU module
Sound
An OPL2 or OPL3 FM chip at 388h: Sound Blaster cards and most clones

Install in three steps

  1. 1Download the zip or the floppy image from the latest release.
  2. 2Run INSTALL from the folder that holds REBEEP.COM, and choose install or update.
  3. 3Add a ReBeep entry to your boot menu that loads Jemm, then ReBeep.
